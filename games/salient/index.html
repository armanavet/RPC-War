Salient
  • Ground belongs to whoever projects more force onto it. Every unit pushes force outwards, falling off with distance. Add both sides up on a square and the larger number owns it. The line between the two colours is the front — nobody draws it, it is just where the numbers cross.
  • You take a unit by surrounding it, not by landing on it. A unit that cannot trace a path home through ground the enemy does not hold is cut off. Two turns later it surrenders. A unit that is simply out-gunned where it stands breaks at once.
  • You may only move units inside your command. Your general and your two staff officers each carry orders a certain distance. Everything outside those circles sits still. Killing a staff officer puts a whole wing to sleep.
  • One unit per turn. That is the whole turn.
  • Score the five towns. At the start of your turn you bank a point for each objective you control — the crossroads counts three. First to 520 wins. Lose your general and you lose immediately.
  • Engineers dig and bridge. An engineer can entrench where it stands, or throw a bridge across a river square beside it.
  • Replacements arrive every five turns and can be placed on any baseline square you still hold.
